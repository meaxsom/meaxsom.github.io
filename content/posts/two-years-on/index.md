In the Last Two Years I’ve:

- Moved from full-remote to a full-on-prem work status
- Reignited my passion for software development and data
- Found renewed energy working in close collaboration with users
- Built strong confidence troubleshooting IT issues
- Strengthened practical AI skills for work and personal use
- Expanded my circle by meeting new people and making new friends
- Rediscovered joy in life and a genuine, familiar smile

What I've Learned:

- I’m fundamentally a developer who enjoys solving real user needs
- Technical problem-solving suits me better than people or process management
- I can quickly learn and apply new skills when needed
- Lower stress offsets trade-offs like less time off or higher pay
- My identity extends well beyond my job or career
- Building internal business software is as fulfilling as product development

Looking back at what I wrote at the [6-month mark](posts/finding-my-way/), getting lost in the problem/need solving still happens, just not as much now that the major - and minor - patterns of FileMaker development have sunk in. A lot of the nagging issues that plagued the system have been addressed by tracking down "root cause" instead of addressing symptoms. Developing an internal logging framework has really helped.

There's a fair amount of my day that's just IT trouble-shooting, e.g. user issues with:

- peripherals - mice, keyboard, monitors, printers, etc.
- network/connectivity
- Microsoft products, primarily Sharepoint sync'ing
- general computer problems - won't boot, freezing, etc.
- installing software, mainly from a enterprise standpoint (`winget` is my friend)

That was expected, but along the way I've learned a lot about what typical users face. Coming from a highly computer literate environment - i.e. software developers, IT issues were solved by the developers - no "help desk". It has been a real eye-opener for just how much day-to-day assistance is required for computer users that are not developers or otherwise familiar with troubleshooting OS, networking, and/or computer connectivity issues. On the plus side, I know way more about Windows that I ever wanted to know.

From an interest standpoint, my competency with [Zoho Assist](https://www.zoho.com/assist/) is pretty good - use it daily. Smartsheet, not so much - I don't even use it for Jira type tasks anymore - and as far as Powershell, well... let's say that it has become a vehicle for understanding how to develop with AI. Describing to the coding-assistant "de jour" what's needed/required - much like a design document for a Jr. developer - and having it write PowerShell scripts keeps me out of the syntax. That process is evolving my AI approach, both as a vector for software development but also AI interactions in general. Perhaps more on that at some point.

Watching this manufacturing company evolve over the past 2 years - understanding where it has come from and where it want's to go - is interesting. In someways it's like watching software evolve - reacting to market pressures/needs/etc. - but in other ways it is also trying to "shape" an aspect of the market at large.

What I wrote [further back](/posts/ahead-of-me/) still holds - the best days are ahead of me, even now at 62.
